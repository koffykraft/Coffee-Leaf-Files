Door Nine · Rest, Stress & Human Response

An early human signal, not a settled benefit.

A 2025 study of green-, black- and oolong-style coffee-leaf teas measured sensory response, GABA, phenolics and physiological variables including cortisol.

Human intervention2025

The oolong-style preparation showed the highest reported phenolic concentration in that study and approximately 0.40 mg/g GABA. Researchers reported a statistically significant reduction in salivary cortisol after the oolong preparation and favourable emotional/sensory responses.

Study DOI →

Interpretation boundary

This is interesting early human evidence. It does not establish that coffee-leaf tea treats stress or anxiety, and it does not justify claims of equivalence to pharmaceutical or supplement interventions. Larger, controlled replication is needed.

What could contribute to the experience?

Chemistry

GABA, phenolics, caffeine and volatile compounds.

Sensory experience

Sweetness, bitterness, astringency, aroma and preference.

Behaviour and context

Warm beverage, pause, expectation and ritual context may all influence perceived relaxation.

Physiology

Cortisol and other measures require controlled replication before causal claims.

An open Buna question

Does a “relaxing” coffee-leaf drink act pharmacologically, sensorially, behaviourally—or through several of these at once?
