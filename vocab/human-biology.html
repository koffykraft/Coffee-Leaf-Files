Door Eight · Vocabulary

Human Biology & Functional Research

Terms used in Door Nine to separate composition, digestion, biological plausibility and actual human evidence.

Bioaccessibility

The fraction of a compound released from the food or beverage matrix during digestion and made available for absorption.

Bioavailability

The amount and form of a compound or metabolite that reaches systemic circulation or a target tissue.

Biotransformation

Chemical change caused by digestion, microbiota or host metabolism.

Metabolite

A compound formed when another compound is chemically transformed in the body or by microorganisms.

Gut microbiota

The community of microorganisms living in the gastrointestinal tract.

Microbiome

The microorganisms, their genes and their ecological context. Often used more broadly than “microbiota”.

Intestinal barrier

The epithelial and associated systems that regulate passage between the gut lumen and the body.

Tight junction

Protein structures connecting neighbouring epithelial cells and helping regulate barrier permeability.

SCFA

Short-chain fatty acid; a group of microbial fermentation products such as acetate, propionate and butyrate.

Bile acid

Host-derived molecules involved in fat digestion and signalling, which can also be transformed by gut microbes.

Enterohepatic axis

The linked circulation and signalling between intestine, bile and liver.

Cortisol

A steroid hormone used in stress-response research. A change in one study is not by itself proof of a therapeutic effect.

Human intervention

A study in which people receive a defined exposure or preparation and outcomes are measured.

Human observational evidence

Human behaviour, exposure or reported outcomes are documented without experimentally assigning the exposure.

Animal model

A whole-organism non-human experiment used to study biological mechanisms or responses.

Cell model

An experiment using cultured cells or cell systems rather than a whole organism.

In-vitro assay

A laboratory test outside a living organism, such as enzyme inhibition or antioxidant-capacity testing.

Functional beverage

A beverage discussed for physiological or functional properties beyond basic hydration or nutrition. The term does not itself prove a health benefit.

Health claim

A statement linking consumption with a health outcome. Such claims require a higher level of evidence than compound presence or preclinical activity.

Post-Cup Transformation

Buna term for chemical and biological transformations occurring after a processed beverage is consumed, including digestion, microbial metabolism, absorption and host metabolism.
