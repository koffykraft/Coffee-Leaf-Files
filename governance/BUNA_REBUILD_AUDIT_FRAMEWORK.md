# Buna Rebuild Audit Framework

Status: ACTIVE

This program treats the existing Buna site as source material for reconstruction, not as an architecture that must be preserved.

## Audit unit

Every published HTML page receives a reconstruction dossier. A page is not accepted merely because its links work or because it renders correctly.

Each dossier must answer the following.

### 1. Intended role

- What is this page supposed to do?
- Who is it for?
- What question should a reader be able to answer after reading it?
- Is it a foundation, introduction, tradition record, experiment, reference, glossary, sensory lesson, culinary page, biology page, tool, archive, or compatibility route?

### 2. Content inventory

Review every meaningful heading, paragraph, sentence, label, table, note, caption and callout.

For each unit determine:

- essential / useful / redundant / misplaced / obsolete;
- new information versus repetition;
- whether the wording is intelligible to a lay reader;
- whether the unit belongs on this page or another page;
- whether an idea needs expansion, compression, splitting or merging.

### 3. Context

- What must a reader know before this page?
- What should follow it?
- What related material belongs elsewhere?
- Does the page explain why its subject matters?
- Does it accidentally assume knowledge that exists only on another page?

### 4. Message-engine integrity

Every substantive statement must be classified where applicable as:

- established fact;
- published experimental result;
- observation;
- traditional/community report;
- interpretation;
- hypothesis;
- recommendation;
- analogy/rhetorical framing.

Then inspect:

- factual validity;
- evidence level;
- certainty calibration;
- claim-to-source fit;
- meaning alignment;
- missing qualification;
- overclaiming or underclaiming;
- inference leakage;
- contradiction;
- whether the likely reader takeaway is stronger or different from what the evidence permits.

A technically true sentence fails if its ordinary reading creates a materially misleading impression.

### 5. Information architecture of the page

- Does the page have one clear job?
- Are ideas in the right order?
- Are sections logically nested?
- Should material be a paragraph, note, table, evidence box, glossary entry, separate page or cross-link?
- Does the page teach progressively?

### 6. Visual representation

Inspect actual rendered behaviour, especially on mobile:

- typography and hierarchy;
- line length and density;
- spacing;
- contrast;
- tables and diagrams;
- sticky/fixed controls;
- horizontal overflow;
- nested scrolling;
- repeated navigation bars;
- visual prominence versus semantic importance.

### 7. Navigation and indexing

Treat these as separate functions rather than interchangeable links:

- global site navigation;
- Door navigation;
- breadcrumb;
- local section index;
- previous/next sequence;
- contextual cross-links;
- back-to-parent action;
- Catalogue/Library Map entry.

Flag duplicated controls even when all links technically work.

### 8. Bibliography, citations and references

- Does every citation point to the intended source?
- Does the cited source support the adjacent claim?
- Is the reference complete enough to identify the work?
- Are source links live and canonical where possible?
- Are bibliography entries duplicated or unused?
- Are citations placed close enough to the claims they support?
- Is evidence provenance visible to the reader at the right level?

### 9. Cross-library comparison

Compare the page with the rest of the corpus for:

- duplicated essays;
- parallel explanations;
- obsolete versions;
- contradictory claims;
- inconsistent terminology;
- repeated introductions;
- competing canonical pages;
- historical material that should be preserved but separated from current guidance.

## Required page disposition

Every page or major content unit must ultimately receive one or more explicit dispositions:

- KEEP
- KEEP WITH EDIT
- REWRITE
- MOVE
- MERGE
- SPLIT
- REFERENCE ONLY
- ARCHIVE
- REDIRECT
- RETIRE

No material is deleted merely because it is awkward or duplicated. Unique content is identified before consolidation.

## Whole-library synthesis

Only after all page dossiers are complete, derive the rebuilt library architecture from the material itself.

The synthesis must define:

1. knowledge domains;
2. canonical content families;
3. page inventory;
4. reader journeys;
5. evidence and claim architecture;
6. terminology model;
7. source and citation model;
8. cross-reference rules;
9. navigation hierarchy;
10. archive/legacy handling;
11. editorial governance.

## Bottom-up rebuild order

After synthesis:

1. skeleton;
2. architectural map;
3. message/evidence architecture;
4. page archetypes;
5. navigation/reference architecture;
6. visual design system;
7. shared technical shell;
8. one representative page per archetype;
9. Door-by-Door migration;
10. full-library integration;
11. two complete examinations after every repair/rebuild unit;
12. final structural, validity/honesty and usability audits.

The old site remains available as the source corpus until the new structure has independently passed acceptance.
