## 1. Intended family role

Kawa Daun is a documented Minangkabau coffee-leaf tradition from West Sumatra. The family should let an ordinary reader understand what it is, then understand the historical/cultural and processing context, then examine the primary research, producer data, processing techniques, uncertainties and source history.

The current six-page family repeats the same colonial-resistance story, processing claims and sensory assertions at different rhetorical intensities rather than at genuine levels of depth.

## 2. Three-depth rebuild disposition

### Level 1 — LOOK: What is Kawa Daun?

Goal: an ordinary reader gets a complete, simple understanding and may stop there.

Keep after verification:
- location: West Sumatra / Minangkabau context;
- coffee leaf as the raw material;
- use of mature Robusta leaves if directly documented;
- heat/smoke drying as a defining documented processing family;
- brewing as a decoction/long extraction if source supports it;
- traditional serving vessel only where useful to understand the practice;
- simple note that the tradition is older than the field study and has a colonial-era history, without turning oral history into documentary certainty.

Do not carry into Level 1 without verification:
- 'predates the Dutch' as settled documentary fact;
- 'survived colonial prohibition';
- 'still made the same way today';
- cinnamon wood as universal/preferred across all producers;
- exact claims about smoke chemistry entering the leaf;
- strength/energy/restorative effects;
- claims that Kawa Daun is a medicine;
- universal claim that all raw material is pruning waste.

Disposition of `kawa-daun-intro.html`: REWRITE → LOOK.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND: Minangkabau context, history and processing

Merge useful material from `kawa-daun.html` and contextual sections of `kawa-daun-deep.html`.

Potentially keep with source checking:

- colonial cultivation system and pankhuis context;
- distinction between community oral history and archival/documentary evidence;

Disposition of `kawa-daun.html`: MERGE / REWRITE → UNDERSTAND.

## 3. Preparation / processing material

Required split:

**Buna/KoffyKraft reconstruction:** any reproducible small-batch method adapted for current use.

Important: a method observed from one producer or district must not be labelled as universal Kawa Daun practice.

Disposition: SPLIT / REFERENCE ONLY pending exact source mapping.

## 4. Schoolbook guide assessment

`kawa-daun-schoolbook-guide.html` uses accessible educational structure but greatly amplifies the historical and functional narrative.

Examples requiring quarantine:
- 'a beverage born from resistance';
- 'warming, energising, restorative';
- 'consumed before physical labour' treated as functional advice rather than reported practice;
- 'existed for centuries' presented as established chronology;
- 'powerful systems tried to erase it';
- pruning waste treated as the universal raw material;
- leaves becoming 'medicine, strength, connection to grandmother's knowledge'.

The format may inspire future Level 1/2 pedagogy, but the claims do not migrate without validation.

Disposition: ARCHIVE SOURCE / DO NOT MIGRATE CLAIMS WHOLESALE.

## 5. Visual guide assessment

`kawa-daun-visual-pictogram-guide.html` presents exact durations, distances, moisture contents, yields and historical labels as visual facts. Some may be valid producer data, but the visual format erases source scope and makes one-producer measurements look like universal tradition rules.

Future visual guides must carry source scope visibly, e.g. 'Observed producer method — Tabek Patah' rather than 'Traditional method' where appropriate.

Disposition: RETIRE after audited replacement exists; preserve during migration.

## 6. Message-engine risks requiring verification

### High priority

1. Exact claim that Kawa Daun predates Dutch rule and the evidence type supporting it.
2. Whether colonial authorities prohibited local bean consumption or instead controlled/depot purchase; exact wording matters.
3. Meaning and operation of `pankhuis` in the cited source.
4. Claims that Minangkabau merchants sold surplus beans to Singapore and Malacca and the source basis.
5. Robusta as the documented species/raw material across the observed producers.
6. Local phrase 'old coffee' and its exact meaning.
7. Whether mature leaves were from pruning, attached branches, detached leaves, or all of these depending on producer.
8. 8–11am collection timing and whether it applies across producers.
9. Lack of washing/sorting and scope of this observation.
10. Three production techniques plus an older fourth method: exact classification and terminology.
11. Smoke/heat duration for each producer method.
12. Bamboo clamping/skewering details.
13. Cinnamon wood preference and which producer/source states it.
14. Claims about smoke volatile chemistry entering leaves and creating finished flavour.
15. 400+ wood-smoke volatile compound claim relevance to Kawa Daun specifically.
16. Final moisture values 3.6–7.6% and which methods/producers they correspond to.
17. Yield/batch figures and whether they are measurements or averages.
18. Storage in bamboo tubes (`perian`) and palm fibre (`ijuk`) scope.
19. Coconut-shell service and variants.
20. Reported strength/energy use before work — exact quotation and source status.
21. Women's role in knowledge transmission and connection to Minangkabau matriliny.
22. Claims of unchanged continuity across generations.

### Rhetorical/interpretive claims to rewrite or label

- 'the bean could be taken; the knowledge of the leaf could not';
- 'born from resistance';
- 'not a story of deprivation';
- 'system that tried to claim the plant';
- 'powerful systems tried to erase it';
- 'smoke is the process' as a universal statement;
- 'cinnamon wood smoke becomes part of leaf chemistry' unless directly measured;
- 'medicine, strength, grandmother's knowledge' as factual synthesis.

These may be useful interpretation or literary framing, but cannot substitute for evidence.
