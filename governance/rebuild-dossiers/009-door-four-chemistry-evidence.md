## 1. Intended role

Door Four should answer one progressively deeper question:

> What is in a coffee leaf, and what can happen to those components when the leaf is processed?

It should make chemistry intelligible to an ordinary reader without pretending that the Citane framework is itself a body of experimental evidence.

Door Four owns chemistry explanation and evidence mechanics. It should not duplicate:
- Door One's first sensory introduction;
- Door Three's practical processing protocols;
- Door Six's sensory learning sequence;
- Door Eight's dictionary function;
- the Foundation page's general evidence policy.

## 2. Fundamental evidence distinction

The current Reactive Landscape uses a conceptual vocabulary including:
- reservoirs;
- activation events;
- energy systems;
- process domains;
- emergent states.

These may be useful as an organizing model, but they must be separated from experimentally established entities and mechanisms.

The rebuild should explicitly distinguish:

**Measured component / published mechanism** — directly supported by research.

**Scientific interpretation** — a reasonable synthesis of published findings.

**Citane model** — an organizing framework created for thinking about processing.

**Hypothesis** — a proposed causal pathway not established in the relevant coffee-leaf system.

A model may be scientifically informed without being experimentally validated as a model.

## 3. Three-depth architecture

### Level 1 — LOOK: What is inside a coffee leaf?

Ordinary-reader page.

Suggested structure:
1. A leaf contains many kinds of substances, not one 'active ingredient'.
2. Simple groups:
   - water;
   - sugars and organic acids;
   - phenolic compounds;
   - caffeine and other alkaloids where relevant;
   - pigments;
   - amino acids/proteins;
   - aroma precursors / volatile compounds.
3. Processing changes availability, concentration, breakdown or interaction of some of these components.
4. Heat, oxygen, time, microbes and physical damage can matter.
5. Different processing therefore creates different cups.
6. `Understand the chemistry →`.

No reservoir names, state codes or reaction-network diagrams at Level 1.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND: How processing can change chemistry

Purpose: explain mechanisms without requiring specialist knowledge.

Possible sections:
- leaf structure and compartmentalisation;
- what happens when cells are damaged;
- role of oxygen;
- role of enzymes;
- role of microbes where demonstrated;
- heat reactions;
- moisture and drying;
- volatile formation/loss;
- extraction into the cup.

Each section should use the pattern:

**plain statement → what research supports → what remains uncertain → optional technical term**.

The Citane model may appear here as an optional explanatory map labelled clearly as a model.

### Level 3 — EXAMINE: Reactive Landscape & evidence

This is where the full framework belongs.

May include:
- reservoirs;
- activation events;
- energy systems;
- process domains;
- emergent states;
- source-by-source mapping;
- biochemical pathways;
- confidence/evidence tables;
- contradictions and missing data;
- full bibliography.

Every framework component must state whether it is:
- directly measured in coffee leaf;
- inferred from coffee-leaf studies;
- borrowed from analogous plant/food systems;
- Citane synthesis;
- hypothesis.

## 4. `citane-reactive-landscape.html` assessment

### Strengths
- contains a coherent conceptual attempt to connect leaf composition, processing and sensory outcome;
- useful as an advanced thinking tool;
- offers a potentially reusable framework for Door Three experimentation;
- already contains evidence/caution/note visual vocabulary.

### Structural problems
- page is far too dense to serve as a Door landing;
- own sticky local navigation collides with injected global navigation;
- contains primer, glossary, framework, evidence tables, state taxonomy and navigation in one large document;
- overlaps processing manual and sensory chemistry;
- visual presentation resembles an established scientific monograph even where parts are framework/synthesis.

### Message-engine risk
The page subtitle calls the framework a `science-based model`. Elsewhere the Catalogue more cautiously says it describes `what may be happening chemically`.

The rebuilt advanced page should prefer wording such as:

**A research-informed Citane model for organizing what published coffee-leaf chemistry suggests may happen during processing.**

That wording preserves utility without upgrading the model into validated mechanism.

Disposition: KEEP AS SOURCE / REWRITE → Level 3.

## 6. Evidence display for ordinary readers

Level 1 should use almost no badges.

Level 2 may use three simple labels:
- **Research shows**
- **Likely / interpreted**
- **Still uncertain**

Level 3 may use the complete taxonomy:
- direct measurement;
- published result;
- analogous evidence;
- Citane synthesis;
- hypothesis;
- unknown.

The evidence system must clarify the reading, not turn every paragraph into metadata.

## 7. Validity queue

The sentence-level audit must verify, among other things:
1. every named compound/reservoir and source;
2. whether concentrations refer to fresh leaf, dried leaf or beverage;
3. leaf-age differences;
4. mangiferin distributions;
5. caffeine variation;
6. chlorogenic acid claims;
7. carotenoid / β-ionone precursor claims;
8. volatile-compound mappings;
9. GABA formation/preservation claims;
10. enzyme activity claims;
11. oxidation mechanisms;
12. microbial fermentation mechanisms;
13. Maillard/caramelisation claims and temperature conditions;
14. pigment/chlorophyll degradation;
15. lipid/aroma relationships;
16. any pathway imported from tea, coffee bean or general food chemistry;
17. whether 'emergent states' are observations, categories or predictions;
18. causal links from chemistry to sensory descriptors.

For each, the audit must record the exact evidence scope.

## 8. Ordinary-reader design

### LOOK
A vertical illustrated leaf with 5–7 component families. Tap/expand optional; no chemical formulas required.

### UNDERSTAND
A simple sequence:

`intact leaf → processing event → chemical change → possible cup effect`

Only a few examples at a time.

### EXAMINE
Full network/model, tables, pathways and references.

The visual language can become more technical as depth increases, but the evidence distinction becomes *more* explicit at the same time.
