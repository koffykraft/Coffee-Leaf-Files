## 1. Intended role

Door Six should teach ordinary people how to notice, describe and understand a sensory experience.

It should not require expertise, and it should not become a second chemistry archive or a second Library Map.

Its strongest existing principle should survive:

> begin with experience, then language, then chemistry; the reader may stop at any layer.

This is closely aligned with the Library-wide three-depth model.

## 2. Three-depth structure

### Level 1 — LOOK / NOTICE

Combine the best of:
- `You Already Sense`;
- `The Encounter`.

Goal:
- reassure ordinary readers that sensory attention is learnable;
- use familiar daily-life anchors;
- let the reader notice aroma, taste, texture, temperature and finish without technical terminology;
- offer one simple cup exercise.

This may remain two short lessons if pacing benefits, but they are one depth level, not separate architectural depths.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND / NAME

Source pool:
- `sensory-language.html`;
- approved material from Sensory Landscape;
- relevant Door Eight vocabulary.

Goal:
- teach useful words;
- distinguish aroma, taste, mouthfeel, astringency, finish;
- show that descriptors are communication tools, not objective chemical identities;
- explain individual variation and context.

### Level 3 — EXAMINE / EXPLAIN

Source pool:
- `sensory-chemistry.html`;
- Door Four chemistry sources;
- primary sensory studies.

Goal:
- connect compounds/process to sensory evidence;
- explain OAV carefully;
- distinguish measured compound concentration from actual human perception;
- show study scope and uncertainty;
- provide references.

Door Six may link to Door Four for full chemistry rather than duplicating the entire Reactive Landscape.

## 3. What the current School gets right

`buna-sensory-school.html` has several strong structural ideas:
- no prior knowledge required;
- experience before terminology;
- reader can stop at any layer;
- calm, non-testing tone;
- attention rather than memorisation.

These should become part of Buna's general ordinary-reader design grammar.

However, the landing says `Four ways in` while conceptually exposing Before You Begin plus three layers. The rebuild should map these to the Library-wide three-depth language rather than inventing a parallel hierarchy.

Disposition: KEEP CONCEPT / SIMPLIFY.

## 4. `sensory-already.html`

### Strengths
- ordinary-life anchors are an effective teaching device;
- reduces intimidation;
- strongly aligned with LOOK level.

### Risks requiring correction/verification
- `hexanal` presented as the cause of the smell of tearing any leaf is too singular/universal;
- mint analogy is then transferred to a claimed coffee-leaf cooling compound/mechanism without sufficient evidence;
- `primary sweet character` linked to an unnamed processed compound as a broad causal claim;
- `most consistently preferred quality across all consumer studies` requires study-by-study support;
- simplification around aroma/taste physiology must remain accurate.

Disposition: KEEP PEDAGOGY / REWRITE → LOOK.

## 5. `sensory-encounter.html`

### Strengths
- attempts to describe experience before naming compounds;
- sensory sequence of aroma, arrival, mid-palate, finish is accessible;
- simple tasting experiment is useful.

### Risks
- statements like `the first thing most people notice` need actual basis or softer wording;
- `Reported` quotations need provenance. If composed examples rather than verbatim study/tasting records, they must not be labelled as reports;
- `aftertaste ... is measurable` is too vague;
- claims about hot/warm/chilled sensory outcomes need exact evidence and product scope;
- `served chilled at 10°C` as best-performing consumer presentation must be traced to a specific study and cannot become universal Buna guidance.

Disposition: MERGE / REWRITE → LOOK.

## 6. `sensory-language.html`

### Strengths
- distinction between experience and vocabulary is good;
- word cards can be effective ordinary-reader teaching components;
- bitterness vs astringency distinction is important.

### Problems / validity queue
- `bitterness is perceived at the back of the tongue` repeats the discredited tongue-map simplification;
- descriptor locations (`tip`, `sides`, `back`) should not be taught as fixed anatomical taste zones;
- `refreshing` is attached to a specific compound/mechanism without adequate visible support;
- some descriptors are described as inherent/non-defect properties when they are contextual judgments;
- claims such as sweetness being the most desirable across research require careful scope;
- `research identified woody as one of three primary descriptors` needs exact study/source and sample context;
- vocabulary claimed to be `specific to this leaf` must distinguish Buna editorial vocabulary from established sensory lexicon.

Disposition: KEEP FORMAT / REWRITE → UNDERSTAND.

## 7. `sensory-chemistry.html`

### Strengths
- begins each compound portrait from sensory experience rather than formula;
- can form the advanced bridge between sensory perception and chemistry;
- attempts to expose OAV and processing relationships.

### High-risk issues
Examples include:
- exact OAV ranges such as β-ionone `132–927` presented broadly as CLT values;
- `one of the primary drivers` of sweet-floral character;
- `12–24 hour wither significantly increases β-ionone`;
- oolong-style `consistently produces the highest β-ionone and sweetest cup`;
- yeast fermentation causal mechanisms;
- leaf-age/process universals;
- compound-to-descriptor causality without sensory interaction context;
- GABA included in a sensory chemistry page even though its relevance to direct sensory perception may be different from aroma/taste compounds;
- PPO framed as a sensory compound rather than an enzyme/process actor.

The advanced rebuild must map each statement to exact study/sample/process conditions.

Disposition: REWRITE → EXAMINE; coordinate with Door Four to avoid duplicated chemistry.

## 8. Sensory Companion

### Current role drift
The Companion currently says it is `A guided walk through the Buna library` and contains a page map spanning traditions, tools, Foundation, research and Catalogue.

That duplicates the global Library Map and turns a learning companion into a site-navigation engine.

### Rebuild role
The Sensory Companion should help the reader:
- notice;
- compare;
- find words;
- ask what a sensation might mean;
- move between the three Sensory School depths.

It may contextually link elsewhere when useful, but should not reproduce all nine Doors.

The Companion must inherit audited content rather than carrying a separate hard-coded mini-library that can become stale.

Disposition: KEEP INTERACTION CONCEPT / REBUILD scope and data source.

## 10. Ordinary-reader visual grammar

Door Six can become a model for the rest of Buna:
- generous reading width;
- short paragraphs;
- one observation at a time;
- familiar examples;
- no dense table on LOOK;
- cards only when they represent genuinely separate concepts;
- technical terms delayed until useful;
- sensory exercise separated visually from factual explanation.

Avoid decorative authority: a beautifully styled card does not make a descriptor scientifically universal.

## 11. Cross-Door ownership

Door Six owns **learning to sense and describe**.

Door One owns **first introduction to the cup**.

Door Four owns **full chemistry/evidence mechanics**.

Door Eight owns **lookup definitions**.

Door Five owns **interactive flavour exploration**.

Therefore:
- Door Six should link to the vocabulary rather than reproduce dictionary-scale definitions;
- Door Six should link to advanced chemistry rather than reproduce every compound record;
- the Flavour Landscape should consume approved sensory concepts rather than maintain an independent descriptor truth set.

## 14. Governing principle

> First help the reader notice something real. Then give it a useful name. Only then explain what might cause it.

The explanation must not be more certain than the evidence, and the vocabulary must not pretend that every person perceives the cup in the same way.
