## 1. Intended family role

Kuti is a documented Harari coffee-leaf tradition. The family should allow an ordinary reader to understand what Kuti is and why fallen leaves matter, then optionally move into cultural/evidence context and finally into source critique, chemistry, unresolved terms and detailed preparation evidence.

The current six-page family does not consistently represent increasing depth. Several pages repeat the same propositions with different rhetoric and certainty.

## 2. Three-depth rebuild disposition

### Level 1 — LOOK: Kuti

Goal: an ordinary reader understands Kuti without technical burden.

Keep, after verification and simplification:
- Kuti is associated with Harar/eastern Ethiopia;
- it is a coffee-leaf beverage distinct from bean coffee;
- documented preparations use mature/yellowed/fallen or dried leaves according to source wording;
- household preparation and use belong to Harari coffee culture;
- preparation is not evidence of therapeutic effect;
- a simple indication that some sources describe plain and spiced/milk variants.

Do not place at Level 1 until verified:
- exact caffeine numbers;
- claims that caffeine is 'returned to the tree';
- 'safe for children' statements;
- claims of centuries of continuous identical preparation;
- medicinal/recovery claims stated as effects;
- assertions that the bean went to market while the leaf stayed home as a universal rule.

Disposition of current `kuti-intro.html`: REWRITE into the Level 1 page. Preserve its accessible tone and visual restraint; remove rhetorical and mechanistic overreach.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND: Kuti in Harari life

Combine useful material now split between `kuti.html` and contextual portions of `kuti-deep.html`.

Potentially keep, with source checking:
- Harar as a walled scholarly/trading city rather than a simple coffee village;
- coffee as part of a diversified agricultural economy;
- documented household storage/use of leaves where source support is exact;
- women and coffee-farm ownership where directly supported;
- whole-plant use including leaf and husk, without romanticising it;
- differences between documented plain Kuti and special/variant preparations;
- cultural reports about use by children, mothers, sick/recovering people presented as reports, not efficacy evidence.

Move out of Level 2 unless needed for comprehension:
- detailed chemistry;
- exact historical tonnage;
- long quotations;
- unresolved linguistic terms;
- reconstruction of mechanisms.

Disposition of current `kuti.html`: MERGE / REWRITE into Level 2.

### Level 3 — EXAMINE: Kuti evidence, chemistry and sources

Use `kuti-deep.html` as the main source pool, not as a page to preserve wholesale.

Keep after validation:
- source history and provenance;
- Harar agricultural context;
- quotation and interpretation separation;
- Qutti / Hasher / Sirriwabun discussion with uncertainty explicit;
- exact preparation documentation by source;
- evidence table distinguishing direct documentation, interpretation and open questions;
- chemistry only where tied to published measurements;
- unresolved terminology and research gaps;
- full bibliography.

Disposition of current `kuti-deep.html`: KEEP AS SOURCE / REWRITE as Level 3.

## 3. Preparation material

`kuti-recipe.html` currently presents reconstructed preparation as confident instruction. It includes exact leaf-selection rules, drying rules, salt timing, boiling duration, statements about bitterness and caffeine, and scalable serving quantities.

This material must be split into:

**Documented preparation** — only what primary/credible sources actually record.

**Buna reconstruction / working method** — if KoffyKraft develops a modern reproducible preparation, it must be labelled as reconstruction or experiment rather than traditional fact.

Do not preserve automatic serving scalers or precise household quantities unless their source basis is established.

Disposition: REFERENCE ONLY pending claim-by-claim validation; rebuild preparation at Level 2 or 3 according to evidence.

## 4. Schoolbook guide assessment

`kuti-schoolbook-guide.html` is accessible in tone but contains serious evidence upgrades.

Examples requiring quarantine:
- 'A guide for caring for children, nursing mothers, the elderly, and those who are healing';
- 'This is how you know it is safe and gentle';
- 'the tree slowly takes back what it gave the leaf';
- 'most of its caffeine has returned to the tree';
- 'safe for a child to drink';
- exact caffeine comparisons presented as settled fact;
- claims that observed community use proves safety;
- statements that long boiling chemically 'breaks down bitter compounds' as an established mechanism.

The schoolbook format is useful as a future Level 1/2 presentation mode, but its present message engine is not safe to migrate.

Disposition: ARCHIVE AS SOURCE MATERIAL / DO NOT MIGRATE CLAIMS WITHOUT VALIDATION.

## 5. Visual guide assessment

`kuti-visual-pictogram-guide.html` repeats the schoolbook's strongest claims in visual form. Visual authority increases the risk because short cards such as 'Safe for children' look definitive even where the underlying evidence is observational or unverified.

The visual guide should not survive as an independent evidence source.

Future rule: any Kuti visual guide is generated only from approved Level 1/2 content and may introduce no proposition not present in the audited text layer.

Disposition: RETIRE AS CURRENT GUIDE after verified replacement exists; preserve in archive until migration is complete.

## 6. Message-engine risks requiring verification

### High priority

1. Exact claim that Kuti contains approximately 10 mg caffeine per litre.
2. Comparators given as green tea 150–300 mg/L and coffee 400–800 mg/L.
3. Biological claim that senescing/fallen leaves return 'most caffeine' to branches/roots/tree.
4. Claim that fallen leaves are therefore safe for children.
5. Claims of use by children under twelve, nursing mothers, elderly, sick/recovering people — verify exact wording, denominator and whether these are source reports.
6. Claim that preparation has remained the same 'for centuries'.
7. Claim that the longer Kuti is boiled, the sweeter/less bitter it becomes, and whether this is documented practice versus chemical inference.
8. Claimed mechanism that prolonged heat degrades bitter phenolics.
9. Exact leaf state: naturally fallen vs yellowed/mature picked/dried leaves — reconcile sources rather than assuming one universal rule.
10. Exact relationship between Harari women’s coffee-farm ownership and Kuti production.
11. Dedicated household rooms for dried coffee-leaf storage.
12. Historical coffee export figures and contextual claims about Harar.
13. Sirriwabun wording and meaning.
14. Hasher/Qishr/Cascara equivalence framing.
15. Kuti Shai ingredients, milk use, spices and occasions.

### Rhetorical claims to remove or explicitly label as interpretation

- 'the leaf is as useful as the bean';
- 'whole-plant knowledge' as proof of mastery;
- 'the bean went to market, the leaf stayed home' as a universal historical statement;
- 'not a poverty drink' unless the page explains the historiographic issue and evidence;
- 'this is how nature designed it';
- 'Harar people recognized this gift centuries ago';
- 'the leaf that falls is wise'.

These may have literary value but should not carry factual weight.

## 7. Navigation / page architecture problems

Current family exposes six peer destinations:
- What is this?
- What's fascinating
- Go deeper
- Recipes
- Schoolbook
- Visual Guide

This violates the depth model because Schoolbook and Visual Guide are alternate presentations, not deeper knowledge states, while Recipe combines documentation and reconstruction.

Rebuild navigation should be no more than:

**Kuti — Look**
→ **Understand Kuti**
→ **Evidence & Sources**

A documented preparation module may appear inside Look/Understand, with a deeper technical preparation appendix only if warranted.

Visual presentation is embedded or offered as an alternate view, not as a peer branch.

## 8. Ordinary-reader design requirements

Level 1 should be visually calm and short:
- one title;
- one sentence identifying place/tradition;
- simple 'what it is' explanation;
- 3–4 verified facts maximum;
- a small documented-preparation diagram if reliable;
- one clearly labelled 'What people report' box if culturally important;
- one 'Understand more' link.

No caffeine bar charts, medicinal benefit cards, or mechanistic diagrams at Level 1.

Level 2 may use maps, household-context cards and comparison tables, but every visual must simplify rather than decorate.

Level 3 can contain evidence tables, quotations, chemistry and bibliography.

## 9. Family rebuild outcome

The Kuti family should be rebuilt from six overlapping pages into a coherent three-depth path.

**LOOK:** What Kuti is.

**UNDERSTAND:** Harari context, household practice, documented preparation and reported uses.

**EXAMINE:** sources, evidence limits, chemistry, historical questions, unresolved terminology, bibliography.

The reader can leave after any level with a coherent understanding. Deeper pages add resolution, not a different version of the story.
