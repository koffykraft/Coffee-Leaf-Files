## 1. Intended role

Door Seven should invite ordinary readers and cooks to explore coffee leaf as a culinary material without confusing:
- traditional coffee-leaf beverages;
- published food chemistry;
- KoffyKraft kitchen observations;
- proposed culinary concepts;
- recipes/reconstructions;
- AI-generated suggestions.

Its primary question is:

> What can a cook learn by treating coffee leaf as an ingredient rather than only as a beverage?

## 2. Three-depth architecture

### Level 1 — LOOK: The leaf at the table

Purpose: ordinary reader / curious cook.

Keep simple:
- coffee leaf can be explored as extract, whole leaf or background seasoning/additive;
- basic kitchen forces: water, salt, fat, acid, heat;
- some uses are documented traditions, while most Buna Culinary work is experimental;
- three or four examples at most;
- no mechanistic promises.

End with:
- `Try a simple preparation →`
- `Understand how the elements behave →`.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND: Elements and methods

Combine simplified, clearly labelled material from:
- `buna-culinary-elements.html`;
- `buna-culinary-concepts.html`.

Teach:
- what a cook is testing when applying salt, fat, acid, water or heat;
- what is general food science;
- what has actually been observed with coffee leaf;
- what is a proposed question for the kitchen.

Every element should distinguish:
**Known generally / Observed with Buna / Proposed for testing**.

### Level 3 — EXAMINE / COOK

Contains:
- tested preparations;
- exact batch records;
- culinary experiments;
- source/evidence notes;
- failures and changes;
- advanced food chemistry;
- provenance of recipes;
- Kitchen Companion with audited knowledge source.

A recipe should carry a status:
- `Documented tradition`;
- `Buna reconstruction`;
- `KoffyKraft trial`;
- `Untested concept`.

## 3. `buna-culinary.html`

### Strengths
- clear premise;
- good ordinary-reader voice;
- explicitly says no health claims;
- three modes provide a useful organizing idea.

### Problems
Mechanisms are often stated more strongly than the family's own theory notices justify.

Examples needing verification/qualification:
- `Salt ... rounds green edges and supports savoury perception`;
- `Fat binds phenolics` in the relevant culinary system;
- `fat can moderate what might otherwise be too intense`;
- acid at high concentration `competes with the leaf and wins` is rhetorical rather than measurable;
- `steam wilts without destroying` is too absolute;
- milk proteins bind phenolics and therefore `the cup becomes gentler`;
- statement that this is `documented in the Engere tradition across nearly 400 households` wrongly risks converting self-reported household tolerance into proof of a milk-protein mechanism;
- whole-leaf eating is proposed without an established safety/culinary evidence layer;
- `coffee leaf has the chemistry` to function like anchovy/miso/soy sauce is a hypothesis, not established culinary equivalence.

Disposition: KEEP CONCEPT / REWRITE → LOOK.

## 4. `buna-culinary-elements.html`

### Strength
Excellent explicit status notice:
- no published culinary reference for the proposed whole-food treatment;
- reasoned observations;
- offered for testing;
- nothing settled.

This framing should become standard for experimental culinary material.

### Risks
The body sometimes becomes more certain than the notice.

Examples:
- baseline sensory profile presented as if universal;
- salt leading to natural lactic fermentation merely by being held long enough;
- specific effects on tannin sensation, savouriness and amino-acid depth;
- fat/acid/heat outcome predictions;
- `hui-gan-like return` terminology imported from another beverage culture without clear need/source;
- statements mixing general food chemistry with coffee-leaf-specific evidence.

Rebuild each element using three columns/rows:
**General culinary principle / Buna observation / Question still to test**.

Disposition: REWRITE → UNDERSTAND.

## 5. `buna-culinary-concepts.html`

### Strength
Also clearly declares concepts untested.

The core idea—new ingredients often enter an existing culinary grammar—is useful as an invitation to experimentation.

### Risks
Historical examples about tapioca, tomato, chilli, miso, tamarind and culinary diffusion require their own historical accuracy if retained.

Some method descriptions generalise across cuisines too broadly (`every serious cooking tradition`, `oldest methods in the world`).

Coffee-leaf questions must remain questions rather than becoming recommendations.

Disposition: KEEP SELECTED CONCEPTS / MERGE → UNDERSTAND; remove essay-scale historical generalisation unless it genuinely helps the reader.

## 6. `buna-culinary-preparations.html`

Current page presents seventeen foundational preparations and asks the reader to progress one variable at a time.

This can be valuable as an experimental workbook, but every preparation needs provenance/status.

Required fields for each preparation:
- status: documented / reconstruction / estate trial / untested;
- date tested;
- leaf material and processing state;
- exact quantities actually used;
- procedure;
- sensory observations;
- safety/handling notes supported by appropriate evidence;
- what changed from previous test;
- what remains unknown.

The page should not call an untested formulation a recipe in the same visual language as a tested one.

Disposition: REBUILD → EXAMINE / COOK workbook.

## 7. Kitchen Companion — hidden content audit

The AI prompt is part of Buna's published message engine even though users do not normally see it.

Current prompt contains high-risk statements including:
- Buna defined specifically as dried Chandragiri leaf from Thumpassery, which may be inappropriate when answering about traditions/species elsewhere;
- GABA may suppress bitterness perception;
- cold serving `measurably reduces bitterness and astringency` as broad guidance;
- extended wet heat makes bitterness decrease and sweetness increase, attributed to Kuti across centuries;
- caffeine `approximately 10 mg per litre at standard preparation`;
- comparison to green tea and coffee;
- `well below EFSA's 80 mg/L safe reference level` — likely category/units/context problem requiring careful verification;
- `This is why children have been given it` — unsupported causal safety inference;
- all-day drinkability with `No jitteriness, no crash point`;
- `The research supports them. The experience confirms them.`;
- direct Maillard and other process mechanisms;
- hard-coded culinary claims that can conflict with corrected public pages.

This is unacceptable as a separate hidden truth set.

### Rebuild requirement
The Kitchen Companion must retrieve/consume the same audited claim/evidence layer as the visible Library.

Its system instruction should contain behavioural rules, not a large independent encyclopedia of factual claims.

The assistant must distinguish:
- documented tradition;
- published result;
- KoffyKraft observation;
- culinary hypothesis;
- generated idea.

Disposition: REBUILD KNOWLEDGE ARCHITECTURE; keep conversational interface only after content source is governed.

## 8. Whole-leaf culinary safety

Door Seven proposes eating the whole coffee leaf in several modes.

This is materially different from documented strained beverages.

Before any whole-leaf recipe is published as practical instruction, the validity/honesty audit must examine:
- food-safety/regulatory status;
- pesticide/agricultural residue issues;
- caffeine and other constituent exposure by whole-leaf dose;
- leaf age/species variation;
- anti-nutritional or irritant constituents;
- microbial handling/fermentation safety;
- storage and moisture;
- whether traditional beverage safety evidence can be transferred to whole-leaf ingestion (it generally cannot be assumed).

Until resolved, whole-leaf preparations should be clearly experimental and not ordinary-reader instructions.
