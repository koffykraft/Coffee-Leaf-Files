## 1. Intended role

Door One is the gentlest threshold into Buna.

An ordinary reader should be able to walk in with no coffee-leaf knowledge, understand the idea of coffee leaf as a beverage, get a simple sense of taste and variation, and walk out satisfied.

If curious, the reader can then move deeper into sensory understanding and finally into research/chemistry.

Door One must not behave like a second homepage or a miniature Catalogue.

## 2. Three-depth architecture

### Level 1 — LOOK: Begin With a Cup

`begin-with-a-cup.html` is the correct conceptual base but needs simplification.

Keep:
- What is coffee leaf?
- Where has it been used?
- What might it taste like?
- How is it different from coffee and tea?
- Why study it?

These five questions are ordinary-reader questions and form a strong Level 1 sequence.

Revise:
- keep each answer short;
- verify broad origin/tradition statements;
- taste descriptors should be framed as variable sensory language, not universal profile;
- avoid requiring the reader to remember tradition names at first contact.

Remove/restructure from Level 1:
- three-column `Cultural Explorer / Sensory Explorer / Processor-Researcher` directory;
- direct links to multiple research papers;
- direct Vocabulary deep links;
- duplicate site-map behaviour.

End Level 1 with only:
- `Back to Library`
- `Understand the cup →`
- optionally `Explore Living Traditions` as a secondary route.

Level 1 should feel complete in itself.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND: The cup and its sensory variation

A simplified sensory page should answer:
- why coffee-leaf cups can taste different;
- how leaf state and processing change aroma/taste/mouthfeel;
- a small set of useful sensory anchors;
- fermented vs non-fermented or other broad processing contrasts only where supported;
- how to taste without requiring chemistry knowledge.

This level should use plain words first and optionally expose technical terms.

Do not put at the top of Level 2:
- exact optimum brewing temperatures/times;
- caffeine comparison numbers;
- compound grids;
- literature-method details;
- dense source labels.

Disposition of `sensory-landscape-of-coffee-leaf.html`: SPLIT / REWRITE. Its approachable sensory explanation belongs at Level 2; its research detail belongs at Level 3 or in shared Door Four/Six references.

### Level 3 — EXAMINE: sensory evidence and chemistry

Door One should not duplicate entire research and sensory-school systems.

Instead Level 3 becomes a controlled bridge to authoritative deeper material:
- sensory evidence and compounds;
- study design and processing comparisons;
- brewing-study details;
- chemistry of aroma/taste;
- full references.

This may be a dedicated `Sensory evidence` page or a curated gateway into Door Four / Door Six, depending on whole-library synthesis.

The governing rule: Door One owns the beginner journey, not all sensory knowledge.

## 4. Message-engine risks in current Sensory Landscape

High-priority verification:

1. `95°C` as 'consumer panel optimum'.
2. `5 min` attributed to Fibrianto et al.
3. `Serve chilled — reduces bitterness and astringency`.
4. `~10 mg/L caffeine` and comparison to green tea 150–300 mg/L.
5. Whether the caffeine number refers to coffee-leaf beverage generally, a particular preparation, or material imported from the Kuti family.
6. `Sweetness dominant in oolong-style` and exact study basis.
7. compound-to-descriptor mappings.
8. fermented/non-fermented sensory differences and sample/product scope.
9. any brewing recommendation that converts a study condition into a universal best practice.

These values may be valid in specific studies, but they should not appear in a quick-reference panel without product/context scope.

## 6. Ordinary-reader page design

### LOOK page

Suggested structure:

1. **Coffee leaves can be brewed too.**
   Two or three sentences.

2. **Where?**
   A tiny map or four place labels, no historical essay.

3. **What can it taste like?**
   6–8 plain sensory words, explicitly variable.

4. **Coffee, tea, or something else?**
   Short distinction.

5. **Why Buna studies it.**
   One paragraph.

6. **Want to go deeper?**
   - `Understand the cup →`
   - `See the living traditions →`

No long page directory.

### UNDERSTAND page

Suggested structure:
- Leaf + processing = cup variation;
- 3–4 broad sensory dimensions;
- simple processing comparison;
- tasting guidance;
- evidence labels only when needed;
- `Evidence & chemistry →`.

### EXAMINE layer

Suggested structure:
- study/source table;
- brewing-study context;
- volatile compounds;
- sensory methods;
- uncertainty;
- references;
- links to Door Four / Door Six canonical deep material.

## 7. Navigation principle

Door One should visibly embody the doorway idea:

**LOOK → UNDERSTAND → EXAMINE**

But it must not force all three.

At Level 1:
- `← Library`
- `Understand more →`

At Level 2:
- `← Simpler`
- `Examine evidence →`

At Level 3:
- `← Understand`
- `Go to Sensory School / Chemistry` where relevant.

No horizontal six-item local navigation strip.

## 9. Governing test

A person who has never heard of coffee-leaf beverages should be able to open Door One on a phone, read for two minutes, understand the subject, and leave without encountering a research-paper interface.

A curious reader should be able to keep walking inward and encounter progressively more precision without the basic explanation changing underneath them.
