# Buna Whole-Library Synthesis

Status: ARCHITECTURE DERIVED FROM 91-PAGE AUDIT

This synthesis asks:

> If the present website did not exist, but all useful material from its 91 HTML pages did, what is the simplest, most truthful, most usable Coffee Leaf Library we would build for an ordinary reader?

This is an architectural synthesis. It does not close the source-by-source validity queues recorded in the page/family dossiers.

---

## 1. The Library's job

Buna is not primarily a product brochure, academic journal, recipe site, health site, glossary or experiment log.

It is a **progressive public knowledge library about coffee leaves as a beverage and food material**.

Its core promise should be:

> You can walk in knowing nothing, understand something useful quickly, and choose whether to leave or go deeper. The deeper you go, the evidence becomes more detailed without the truth changing underneath you.

---

## 2. The one structural law

Every knowledge path follows:

**LOOK → UNDERSTAND → EXAMINE**

### LOOK — Basic
For an ordinary reader.

- plain language;
- one clear question;
- short enough to finish comfortably on a phone;
- gives a complete basic understanding;
- no obligation to continue.

### UNDERSTAND — Intermediate
For the curious reader.

- context;
- variation;
- examples;
- evidence distinctions;
- explanations of why something matters.

### EXAMINE — Advanced
For serious inquiry.

- source methods;
- technical detail;
- chemistry;
- exact measurements;
- limitations;
- contradictions;
- bibliography;
- research/experimental records.

The three levels must describe the same underlying reality at increasing resolution.

---

## 3. Doors survive — but as thresholds, not filing cabinets

The nine-Door idea is structurally useful because each Door represents a different question a reader may ask.

The mistake in the old site was treating each Door as a collection of accumulated pages rather than a threshold into a depth path.

The rebuilt Doors should therefore survive in simplified form.

### Door One — Begin With a Cup
**Question:** What is coffee leaf as a drink?

Owns:
- first orientation;
- simple cup description;
- broad sensory variation;
- difference from bean coffee / Camellia tea without overcomplication.

Does not own:
- full chemistry;
- sensory training;
- tradition histories;
- research papers.

### Door Two — Living Traditions
**Question:** Where and how do communities actually use coffee leaves?

Owns:
- Engere;
- Kuti;
- Chemo;
- Kawa Daun;
- cultural/household context;
- documented traditional preparations;
- community reports as reports.

Does not own:
- clinical interpretation;
- modern KoffyKraft recipes presented as tradition;
- general chemistry.

### Door Three — Processing & Experiment
**Question:** What can people do to a leaf, and what changes when they do it?

Owns:
- processing choices;
- process compass;
- protocols;
- Citane frameworks;
- experiments;
- field notes;
- measured observations;
- reconstruction methods.

Must distinguish:
- published knowledge;
- documented tradition;
- Citane framework;
- Citane observation;
- hypothesis.

### Door Four — Inside the Leaf: Chemistry & Evidence
**Question:** What is in the leaf and how might processing change it?

Owns:
- composition;
- chemical families;
- reactions/mechanisms;
- evidence mechanics;
- Reactive Landscape as a clearly labelled Citane model.

Does not own:
- processing instructions;
- sensory curriculum;
- health outcomes.

### Door Five — Explore With Tools
**Question:** Can I explore the Library interactively?

Owns:
- Terrain Map;
- Flavour Landscape;
- Logic Board;
- Epsilon/speculative tool.

Tools consume shared evidence objects. They are never independent evidence sources.

### Door Six — Sensory School
**Question:** How do I notice and describe what is in the cup?

Owns:
- sensory attention;
- sensory vocabulary in use;
- tasting exercises;
- perception explanations;
- Sensory Companion.

Depth language can naturally read:
**NOTICE → NAME → EXPLAIN**.

### Door Seven — The Leaf at the Table
**Question:** What can a cook do with coffee leaf?

Owns:
- culinary exploration;
- elements/methods;
- tested preparations;
- experimental cooking;
- Kitchen Companion.

Must distinguish:
- documented traditional food/beverage use;
- KoffyKraft trial;
- reconstruction;
- untested concept.

### Door Eight — Vocabulary
**Question:** What does this word mean here?

Owns:
- canonical definitions;
- term provenance;
- related terms;
- technical definitions;
- project-specific terminology.

It is a service used by the entire Library, not fifteen sequential mini-books.

### Door Nine — After the Cup: Human Biology
**Question:** What happens in the body, and what does research actually show?

Owns:
- digestion;
- bioavailability/exposure;
- gut/barrier/metabolism;
- human-response evidence;
- safety/dose;
- research translation;
- regulatory references.

Its evidence discipline should become a model for the entire Library.

## 4. Foundation is not a Door

`The Foliage of Buna` should remain a **Foundation essay**, not compete with Door One or Door Two.

Its job:
- explain why coffee leaf belongs in a knowledge library;
- give the historical/ethnobotanical frame;
- explain how Buna treats evidence;
- explain how to use the Library.

It should not contain:
- another full Library Map;
- duplicate navigation;
- recipe/process detail;
- health claims;
- large inventories of every page.

Home may link to Foundation as `Why this Library exists`.

---

## 5. Home is a threshold, not a catalogue

The homepage should answer only:

1. What is this Library?
2. Where should I begin?
3. What are the nine Doors?
4. How do I find a specific term/page/source?

Suggested Home structure:

### Hero
**A library about brewing and understanding coffee leaves.**

One sentence of scope.

Primary action:
**Begin with a cup**.

Secondary:
**See all Doors**.

### Nine Doors
Compact cards with:
- question;
- one sentence;
- `Walk in`.

### Utility links
- Search / Vocabulary;
- Catalogue;
- Sources;
- About / Foundation.

No professional-reader mini-navigation before the basic reader has understood the site.

---

## 6. Catalogue has one job

Catalogue is the complete structured inventory for readers who want the map.

It should be generated from the same page manifest that powers navigation.

It does not invent its own taxonomy.

Each current canonical page appears once with:
- Door/domain;
- depth;
- page type;
- status;
- short description.

Legacy redirects and archives do not appear in the public Catalogue.

---

## 7. Page archetypes

The rebuild does not need 91 independent designs.

It needs a small set of reusable page archetypes.

### A. Door Landing
Purpose: choose a path.

Contains:
- Door identity/question;
- 2–5 choices maximum;
- no dense essay.

### B. LOOK Page
Purpose: basic ordinary-reader understanding.

Contains:
- one question;
- short explanation;
- 3–5 key facts;
- optional simple visual;
- one evidence caution if essential;
- outward and deeper routes.

### C. UNDERSTAND Page
Purpose: context and meaning.

Contains:
- short orientation;
- contextual sections;
- simple evidence labels;
- diagrams/tables only when they simplify;
- related links;
- simpler/deeper controls.

### D. EXAMINE Page
Purpose: advanced evidence.

Contains:
- methodology/source basis;
- detailed data;
- evidence classification;
- technical explanations;
- uncertainty;
- references.

### E. Field / Experiment Record
Purpose: record what was actually done.

Contains:
- question;
- setup;
- procedure;
- measurements;
- observations;
- interpretation/hypothesis;
- what was not measured;
- next experiment.

### F. Term Detail
Purpose: canonical definition.

Contains:
- plain definition;
- context;
- technical/provenance expansion.

### G. Tool
Purpose: interactive exploration.

Contains:
- simple use mode;
- assumptions/evidence mode;
- logic/provenance mode.

### H. Source Record
Purpose: canonical research/regulatory source.

Contains:
- citation;
- evidence type;
- material/preparation;
- model/population;
- findings;
- limits;
- claims/pages using it.

### I. Legacy Redirect
Purpose: compatibility only.

No public navigation role.

## 8. One shared message engine

The biggest content defect in the old site is not merely repeated prose. It is **repeated propositions with different certainty**.

Examples found in the audit:
- traditional respondent report became health instruction in schoolbooks;
- cautious thermal observation became `single most important fact` in a visual board;
- corrected public pages coexisted with older stronger claims in AI prompts;
- tradition facts were copied into Vocabulary and remained stale.

The rebuild therefore requires one canonical claim/evidence layer.

Conceptually every substantive claim has:

```text
claim_id
statement
plain_statement
scope
claim_type
evidence_level
source_ids
confidence / review_state
limitations
related_terms
related_claims
last_reviewed
```

Claim types may include:
- FACT / DEFINITION
- PUBLISHED RESULT
- DOCUMENTED TRADITION
- COMMUNITY REPORT
- BUNA/CITANE OBSERVATION
- INTERPRETATION
- HYPOTHESIS
- RECOMMENDATION

Pages, tools, visual boards and AI companions all consume the same accepted objects.

---

## 9. Source architecture

Sources should also exist once.

A source record contains:

```text
source_id
citation
doi_or_url
source_type
year
material_or_preparation
study_model_or_population
methods_summary
key_findings
limits
verified_status
```

A claim links to one or more source records.

A bibliography is then a **view** of the sources used on that page, not a manually duplicated list.

This solves:
- unused references;
- claims with unclear source;
- inconsistent titles;
- copied citations;
- source changes not propagating.

---

## 10. Vocabulary architecture

Terms exist once in a term registry.

Pages may show an inline short explanation but link to the canonical term.

Conceptual fields:

```text
term_id
term
plain_definition
category
technical_definition
project_specific_usage
related_terms
source_ids
review_state
```

Tools and AI use the same term record.

## 11. Navigation architecture

There are only five navigation functions in the rebuilt site.

### 1. Global navigation
At site level:
- Home / Library
- Doors / Library Map
- Vocabulary/Search
- Catalogue

On mobile this should be one compact control, not a scrolling horizontal strip.

### 2. Door context
Shows where the reader is:
`Living Traditions / Kuti`

### 3. Depth navigation
Simple progression:
`LOOK · UNDERSTAND · EXAMINE`

or natural equivalent such as:
`NOTICE · NAME · EXPLAIN` in Sensory School.

### 4. Local section index
Only for a genuinely long page, visually subordinate.

### 5. Context links
Related material inside prose, used sparingly.

There should not be separate Back-to-Library, local Library item, global Library item and embedded Library Map all competing on one mobile screen.

---

## 12. Citation and evidence presentation

Evidence should be proportional to depth.

### LOOK
Minimal labels:
- Research shows
- People report
- Still uncertain

Only where necessary.

### UNDERSTAND
More explicit:
- Documented
- Community report
- Interpretation
- Hypothesis

### EXAMINE
Full source/evidence type and direct links.

Citation should sit close enough to the proposition that the reader knows what it supports.

A source list at the bottom cannot repair an ambiguous claim above.

---

## 13. Visual design follows semantic role

The old site often uses styling before epistemic role is settled.

The rebuild reverses that order.

Visual hierarchy should encode:
- depth;
- evidence type;
- page function;
- action hierarchy.

Not every Door needs a unique page layout.

A restrained accent colour/illustration may give a Door character, but layout grammar remains consistent.

Important rules:
- one primary reading column;
- phone first;
- no nested horizontal scrolling nav;
- no duplicated sticky bars;
- no decorative data visual without provenance;
- no precision-looking number without scope/source;
- no box/card merely because content can fit in one.

---

## 14. AI architecture

AI assistants are not independent encyclopaedias.

The old Kitchen Companion and other tools demonstrate the danger of a hard-coded prompt becoming a hidden, stale truth set.

Rebuild rule:

**System prompt = behaviour and safety rules.**

**Knowledge = retrieved/shared audited content objects.**

AI output must distinguish:
- source-derived statement;
- interpretation;
- proposal/hypothesis.

It must not generate health treatment claims or upgrade tradition into proof.

---

## 15. Tool architecture

Tools consume the same claims/terms/sources as pages.

A line in a graph is a claim.
A disabled option is a claim.
A number is a claim.
An AI pathway is a proposal.

Every tool relationship has provenance/evidence semantics.

---

## 16. Archive and legacy architecture

The rebuilt public site should have fewer canonical pages than the current 91-file corpus.

Old URLs fall into:
- current canonical replacement;
- legacy redirect;
- archive source;
- prototype/design source.

Unique old content is captured before retirement.

The public Catalogue counts only current canonical content.

---

## 17. Expected reduction in canonical content

The exact final count should emerge during rebuild, but the audit strongly suggests substantial consolidation.

Examples:
- Door Two falls from roughly 27 tradition-related pages to approximately 13 canonical pages (Door landing + 3 depths x 4 traditions), subject to technical modules.
- Schoolbook and pictogram pages become presentation modes or archives, not parallel truth paths.
- Vocabulary falls from fifteen numbered mini-books to a search/category service backed by term records.
- Door Nine can reduce seven equal peer pages into one LOOK page, four UNDERSTAND modules and advanced evidence/source views.

Reduction is not deletion of knowledge. It is removal of duplicate representations of the same knowledge.

## 19. Bottom-up rebuild sequence

Now that whole-library synthesis exists, implementation should proceed in this order:

### Layer 0 — Frozen source corpus
Current site remains available until replacement acceptance.

### Layer 1 — Skeleton / manifest
Define:
- Doors;
- page IDs;
- page archetypes;
- depth;
- canonical routes;
- ownership.

### Layer 2 — Evidence/data architecture
Define:
- claims;
- sources;
- terms;
- tradition records;
- experiment records.

### Layer 3 — Shared technical shell
Define:
- layout;
- navigation;
- breadcrumb/context;
- depth controls;
- citation rendering;
- evidence callouts.

### Layer 4 — Representative archetypes
Build one:
- Door landing;
- LOOK page;
- UNDERSTAND page;
- EXAMINE page;
- experiment record;
- term detail;
- source record;
- tool shell.

### Layer 5 — Validate archetypes
Ordinary-reader usability + mobile + evidence honesty + accessibility.

### Layer 6 — Migrate one Door at a time
Only audited/verified content is allowed into the new canonical pages.

### Layer 7 — Integrate tools/AI
After shared evidence layer exists.

### Layer 8 — Global Catalogue / search / Library Map
Generated from canonical manifest.

### Layer 9 — Legacy redirects
Map every retired URL intentionally.

### Layer 10 — Full system audit
Structural + content + validity/honesty + usability.

---

## 20. Governing sentence

> Buna should feel simple at the surface, coherent in the middle, and rigorous at depth.

The same Library must serve the person who wants two minutes of understanding and the person who wants to inspect the paper, without giving those two readers different truths.
