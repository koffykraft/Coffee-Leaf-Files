## 1. Intended role

Door Eight should be Buna's **shared language service**.

Its purpose is not to teach every subject in the Library. Its purpose is to make sure that when Buna uses a word, the reader can find one clear meaning and, if desired, inspect its context and evidence.

The governing rule is:

> A definition should exist once. Other Doors may explain a term briefly in context, but they should not maintain competing definitions of the same term.

## 2. Current category error

The present Vocabulary combines at least four distinct products:

1. **Dictionary / glossary** — terms such as withering, astringency, cultivar, decoction.
2. **Citane operational frameworks** — `How to Read a Leaf`, `Quality at Source`, process principles.
3. **Technical teaching/reference essays** — plant care, phenology, thermal transformation, process shaping.
4. **Editorial / proposed use frameworks** — Ritual Context, Kerala daypart/use categories, environmental mission language.
5. **Visual/icon system** — master boards and category diagrams.

These should not all be peer 'vocabulary sections'.

## 3. Three-depth term architecture

The three-depth model applies *within each term*, rather than requiring three separate pages per word.

### Level 1 — LOOK: Plain definition

Every term begins with one ordinary-language definition, ideally one or two sentences.

Example pattern:

**Withering**
Letting a harvested leaf lose some moisture before the next processing step.

No chemistry, recommendation or process doctrine is required to understand the word.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND: Context

Optional expansion:
- where the term is used in Buna;
- simple example;
- related terms;
- what it should not be confused with;
- whether the term is general science, traditional terminology, Buna/Citane terminology or sensory language.

### Level 3 — EXAMINE: Technical meaning and provenance

Optional expansion:
- technical definition;
- exact scope;
- source(s);
- evidence status;
- alternate usage;
- disputed meaning;
- source-specific numbers where necessary;
- date/version if a Citane-created term evolves.

A vocabulary page should therefore be searchable and expandable, not a sequence of mini-essays disguised as cards.

## 4. Recommended term record

Each canonical term should conceptually contain:

- **Term**
- **Plain definition**
- **Category**
- **Used in** (Door/page contexts)
- **Related terms**
- **Do not confuse with**
- **Evidence / provenance class**
- **Technical note**
- **Source(s)**
- **Buna/Citane-specific usage**, if any
- **Version / review status** for project-created terminology

This same record can feed pages, tooltips, search, AI companions and interactive tools.

## 5. Vocabulary Index assessment

### Strengths
- clear intention to create shared language;
- audience includes farmers, processors, café staff and researchers;
- evidence classification is already an explicit design goal.

### Structural problems
- Index says **15 sections**;
- many individual pages still identify themselves as sections `of 14`;
- Ritual Context identifies itself inconsistently as `Section 13` / `13 of 13`;
- this is evidence of architecture growing without one canonical manifest.

The rebuild should not number arbitrary sections at all unless the number has genuine reader value.

More importantly, the current Index describes whole frameworks as vocabulary categories. The new index should instead expose **search + a handful of semantic categories**.

Possible categories:
- Plant & leaf
- Growing environment
- Processing
- Brewing
- Sensory
- Chemistry
- Traditions & cultural terms
- Evidence & research
- Environment / production
- Buna/Citane project terms

No need for fifteen numbered chapters.

## 6. Plant/source vocabulary assessment

### `plant-source.html`
Contains useful terms but definitions often add quality judgements or processing recommendations.

Examples requiring separation:
- young leaf `higher in certain bioactive compounds`;
- intermediate leaf `often optimal for specific Buna preparations`;
- mature leaf `higher in tannins` and `primary material for most pathways`;
- old leaf `generally not used for premium Buna`;
- broken leaf extracting `faster and less uniformly`;
- shade vs sun leaf chemical/quality claims.

Plain botanical/operational definitions can survive. Product-quality conclusions belong to context/evidence layers.

### `leaf-origin.html`
This is closer to an explanatory agronomy page than vocabulary.

High-risk examples:
- higher altitude `concentrates compounds` and produces higher TPC/more complex sensory profiles;
- terroir has `measurable chemical consequences`;
- rubber shade causes lower bitterness;
- wind strengthens tissue/reduces pest pressure as specific Buna factors;
- `no step in processing can create quality not established in origin` as doctrine rather than definition.

Most of this should move to a future plant/growing knowledge family or Door Three/Four context. Vocabulary retains terms such as altitude, terroir, humidity and shade with neutral definitions.

## 7. Plant Variety / Anatomy assessment

`plant-variety.html` contains both useful definitions and significant value claims.

Examples:
- heirloom varieties described as complex, resilient and carrying `memory of place`;
- improved varieties `trade genetic diversity for predictability`;
- clonal production `reduces genetic resilience over time`;
- apical buds/young leaves `highest/rich in active compounds`;
- mature leaves `balanced flavours, more body, denser compound profile`;
- Citane identity `unique — no other producer can replicate it`.

These are not dictionary definitions and require evidence/qualification.

There is also a concrete media defect: the page links to `plant-variety.png` but the `<img>` source is `plant-care.png`. This must be corrected in the eventual rebuild/source cleanup.

## 8. Plant Care assessment

`plant-care.html` is not vocabulary. It is a farm-management primer with project philosophy.

Terms such as nursery, pruning, mulching, IPM, soil health and water management can have vocabulary entries, but statements such as:
- `a poorly managed nursery sets a ceiling`;
- `clean fields produce healthier plants`;
- `healthy plants have natural resistance`;
- `highest-return habits`;
- `what gets measured gets improved`;
are guidance/rhetoric rather than definitions.

Disposition: extract neutral terms into Vocabulary; move agronomic guidance to a future production/growing reference if whole-library synthesis warrants it.

## 9. Seasons & Phenology assessment

The term **phenology** itself belongs in Vocabulary.

The rest is largely a plant-cycle teaching page.

Claims requiring later verification include:
- Kerala monsoons directly determining Citane flush rhythm;
- dry-season leaves becoming more concentrated;
- energy competition between ripening fruit and leaf quality;
- partial dormancy in Kerala;
- warm lowland conditions creating a `fast-moving phenological rhythm`;
- day length influence in near-equatorial Kerala.

Disposition: keep simple term definitions; move plant-cycle explanation to appropriate growing/plant context.

## 10. `How to Read a Leaf`

This page explicitly describes itself as a **Citane Field Framework**.

It is therefore not Vocabulary.

The six questions — Age / Origin / Health / Season / Condition / Intent — may be an excellent processing/field assessment tool, likely Door Three or a future producer workspace.

Its strongest language should also be reviewed:
- healthy leaf `chemically balanced and predictable`;
- `the cup often reveals what the eye ignored`;
- season is `an active ingredient`;
- every pathway decision becomes easier after the six dimensions.

Disposition: MOVE framework out of Door Eight; Vocabulary retains definitions of the six underlying terms.

## 11. `Quality at Source`

Also not Vocabulary. It is a quality doctrine / operational framework.

Terms such as traceability, uniformity, contamination/purity, freshness and integrity may enter the dictionary.

Statements such as:
- selective harvest is `the most impactful quality decision`;
- aroma potential `cannot be created, only unlocked or lost`;
- freshness has `maximum flavour potential` at harvest;
- `no process can create quality not already present`;
are hypotheses/principles requiring context, not definitions.

Disposition: MOVE framework to production/processing quality architecture; extract neutral terms.

## 12. Process vocabulary

`process.html` is closest to a useful glossary category but still turns definitions into claims.

Examples needing verification/qualification:
- bruising vs rolling as separate controlled categories;
- oxidation degree deterministically defining green/black styles;
- microbial fermentation creating compounds `not accessible` otherwise;
- steaming `locks the leaf in its current chemical state`;
- Kuti using pan-firing;
- cinnamon wood as universal/traditional Kawa Daun material;
- sun-drying producing `white-style Buna`;
- shade drying retaining more delicate volatiles;
- decoction being traditional for all four listed traditions;
- extended decoction reducing bitterness/astringency;
- cold brew reducing bitterness/astringency.

Vocabulary should define operations neutrally; process outcomes belong in Door Three/Four evidence.

## 13. Process Shaping

This is a **process architecture**, not vocabulary.

The ten-step journey is useful as Door Three material but should not be treated as a universal linear process because several traditions skip/reorder steps.

Critical inherited overclaims from Door Two appear here:
- Engere `safety record` language;
- Kuti minimum 30 minutes + longer boil reduces bitterness;
- Kawa Daun `predates Dutch colonial period` and cinnamon wood as general rule.

This demonstrates why one shared claim/evidence source is needed: old claims were copied into Vocabulary and survive even after tradition pages became more cautious.

Disposition: MOVE process architecture to Door Three; retain process terms in glossary.

## 14. Thermal Transformation

This is an advanced Door Three/Four technical framework, not vocabulary.

High-priority verification:
- low heat 40–70°C enzyme behaviour;
- PPO arrest `70–85°C` as a fixed range;
- drying `85–100°C` as a general stage;
- Maillard onset `~120°C` and applicability to coffee leaf under these conditions;
- trigonelline degradation at 140–170°C;
- CGA lactone formation;
- pyrazines and sensory implications;
- `170°C+` as useful/damage boundary;
- 70°C/4h claimed as research standard across named studies;
- Kuti pan roasting attribution;
- smoke volatile absorption.

Disposition: MOVE technical framework to Door Three/Four; keep `enzyme arrest`, `Maillard reaction`, `drying`, etc. as neutral glossary terms.

## 15. Sensory vocabulary

### Strengths
This is the category most naturally suited to Door Eight.

It already attempts to separate Research / Observed / Hypothesis.

### Problems
Definitions often conflate:
- descriptor meaning;
- compound association;
- process cause;
- consumer preference;
- product recommendation.

Examples:
- Sweet defined as dominant positive attribute and primary consumer-preference driver;
- Bitter linked to particular compounds and `optimised parameters`;
- Honey-like declared characteristic aroma of β-ionone and `dominant aroma compound`;
- Fermentation/process changes asserted in descriptor definitions;
- Toasted, nutty, vanilla, caramel use general Maillard/ester inference as term content;
- Stone Fruit mixes coffee-flower data with leaf sensory data.

A descriptor definition should first explain **what the word means in sensory use**. Evidence about whether Buna has shown that descriptor is a separate field.

### Mouthfeel / finish
Also suitable for Vocabulary but contains effect claims such as:
- TPC determining body;
- cold serving reducing drying;
- `calming` associated with GABA and low caffeine;
- `uplifting` inferred from caffeine;
- fixed mechanisms for `structured` mouthfeel.

Emotional/functional words such as calming, uplifting, comforting and grounding may be contextual descriptors, but must not masquerade as physiological effects.

## 16. Ritual Context

This page is fundamentally misclassified.

It creates proposed use categories:
- Morning
- Midday
- Evening
- Night
- Alone
- Shared
- Ceremony
- Travel
- Work / Focus
- Rest / Reflect

and then gives preparation/caffeine/function recommendations.

Examples requiring quarantine:
- morning Buna `gently stimulating`;
- midday `nourishing rather than stimulating`;
- evening `lower in caffeine` via longer brew/older leaf;
- night cold brew/light infusion as `minimal caffeine`;
- work/focus `supports rather than distracts`;
- rest/reflect `calming`;
- specific brewing recommendations by time of day.

These are proposed service/product contexts, not vocabulary definitions and not established tradition.

The page also mixes Ethiopian tradition with newly invented Kerala registers, which risks cultural blurring.

Disposition: REMOVE from Vocabulary canonical structure. Preserve as source material for a future Kerala/service-experiment context only if it survives validity and design review.

## 17. Environment & Impact

This page mixes neutral terms with sustainability claims and mission statements.

Vocabulary-worthy:
- biodiversity;
- agroforestry;
- shade system;
- carbon footprint;
- soil carbon;
- traceability.

Not definitions:
- rubber-shade system supports greater biodiversity than monoculture unless measured/scoped;
- `significantly lower footprint` for leaf tea vs bean coffee;
- leaf tea uses less water than wet-process coffee;
- leaf is pruning waste that becomes value;
- `Positive Impact` as a mission;
- specific Thumpassery carbon/biodiversity benefits without data.

Disposition: extract neutral definitions; environmental performance claims belong in an evidence-backed sustainability/production section if retained.

## 18. Master Reference and visual boards

The `Master Reference` is an icon-system guide, not a vocabulary depth.

Evidence labels applied to icon categories such as `Plant Icons — Observed` are semantically weak: an icon category is not itself an observation.

Visual boards must be generated from canonical term records. They cannot maintain parallel descriptions/evidence labels.

Representative Sensory Board shows the risk clearly:
- short visual cells merge descriptors, compounds, processing and evidence into one apparent fact;
- some terms are marked documented where only part of the card's proposition may be documented.

Disposition: keep icon system as **presentation/navigation layer**, fed by the canonical terminology data.

## 19. Navigation architecture

Current structure is fifteen numbered section pages, each with:
- breadcrumb;
- previous/index/next band near top;
- repeated previous/index/next band near bottom;
- global Buna nav injected at runtime;
- often image/board links and connected-section links.

This is excessive for a glossary.

Rebuild:

**Door Eight — Vocabulary**

Primary interface:
- Search terms
- Browse categories
- Recent/related terms only if useful

Click a term → expandable/detail view with:

`Definition`
→ `Context`
→ `Evidence & sources`

The user should not need to walk sequentially through Section 01 → 02 → 03 simply to look up a word.

## 20. Shared-data architecture

Door Eight is a strong candidate to become the first consumer of the future **claim/term data layer**.

Conceptually:

```text
term_id
canonical_term
plain_definition
category
technical_definition
context
related_terms
source_ids
evidence_class
project_specific_usage
review_status
version
```

Other pages/tooltips/AI assistants link to this canonical object.

That removes duplicated definitions from page HTML and reduces semantic drift.

## 21. Ordinary-reader design

### LOOK
Search result or term card:
- term;
- one-line meaning;
- optional icon.

### UNDERSTAND
Expand:
- example;
- where it appears in Buna;
- related / don't confuse with.

### EXAMINE

- source/provenance;
- evidence status;
- project-specific caveat.

No huge term grid by default on a phone.

## 24. Governing principle

> Vocabulary tells the reader what a word means. It does not use the definition as a hidden place to teach an unverified theory, sell a quality doctrine, prescribe a ritual, or make an environmental claim.

Door Eight should become one of the simplest Doors to use and one of the strictest places in the Library about semantic precision.
