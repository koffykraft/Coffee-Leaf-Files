## 1. Intended family role

Chemo is a documented Southwestern Ethiopian coffee-leaf beverage tradition. The family should let an ordinary reader first understand what the drink is, then understand its household/cultural context and documented variation, and finally examine the source methods, ingredients, uncertainties, chemistry and bibliography.

The current seven-page family does not map cleanly to increasing depth. It contains parallel presentation modes and repeated propositions with different levels of certainty.

## 2. Three-depth rebuild disposition

### Level 1 — LOOK: What is Chemo?

Goal: a short, ordinary-reader page.

Keep after verification:
- documented location and community context;
- coffee leaf as the base ingredient;
- spices/herbs as an important part of the documented preparation;
- multiple leaf-preparation methods if directly supported;
- social/hospitality role if source wording supports it;
- food pairing if documented, stated as practice rather than physiological requirement.

Do not state at Level 1 without verification:
- that spices are universal with 'no exceptions';
- that Chemo is 'never' served alone;
- that food moderates physiological intensity;
- that roasting necessarily creates Maillard products in the actual leaf preparation;
- exact percentages and timings unless needed and directly sourced;
- exact leaf-age preference;
- claims that roasting makes the drink sweeter/deeper through a proven chemical mechanism.

Disposition of `chemo-intro.html`: REWRITE → LOOK. Preserve direct tone, remove absolutist and mechanistic language.

### Level 2 — UNDERSTAND: Chemo in household and cultural life

Merge useful material from `chemo.html`, contextual parts of `chemo-deep.html`, and carefully verified plant information.

Potentially keep:
- Tepi Town / Sheka Zone context;
- study population and documented preparation diversity;
- hospitality/social context;
- gender/knowledge transmission if directly documented;
- food pairing as cultural practice;
- overview of herbs/spices and their documented local names;
- comparison of roasted, lightly heated and fresh-leaf methods where source supports the categories;
- cultural pressure/change on the tradition if documented.

Avoid repeating the Level 1 definition. Level 2 should explain why the practice matters and how it varies.

Disposition of `chemo.html`: MERGE / REWRITE → UNDERSTAND.

### Level 3 — EXAMINE: evidence, methods, plants and sources

Use `chemo-deep.html` and `chemo-plants.html` primarily as source pools.

Keep after validation:
- study design, dates, sample size and source provenance;
- exact household percentages and denominators;
- full ingredient record with local/scientific names where verified;
- exact preparation documentation by method;
- evidence table distinguishing direct documentation and interpretation;
- source-specific discussion of Majang/fresh-leaf preparation where supported;
- unresolved questions;
- chemistry only when tied to published measurements or explicitly labelled analogy/hypothesis;
- full bibliography.

Disposition:
- `chemo-deep.html`: REWRITE → EXAMINE.
- `chemo-plants.html`: MERGE into Level 2/3 plant module; not a peer family depth.

## 3. Preparation material

`chemo-recipe.html` presents a highly operational reconstruction with serving scaler, exact gram weights, timings, leaf selection, spice rules, storage/serving rules and mechanistic explanations.

The page itself says quantities are averages across households, but other parts of the page convert that variability into prescriptive rules.

Required split:

**Documented preparation:** exactly what the ethnographic source records.

**Buna/KoffyKraft reconstruction:** a reproducible modern working method, explicitly labelled as reconstruction or experiment.

Do not call reconstruction details 'traditional' merely because they were derived from averages.

Disposition: SPLIT / REFERENCE ONLY until claim-by-claim verification.

## 4. Plant module assessment

`chemo-plants.html` is potentially valuable but mixes four proposition classes inside each card:
- documented ingredient identity/quantity;
- sensory interpretation;
- phytochemistry;
- traditional medicinal association.

Those must be separated visually and semantically.

Examples requiring verification:
- young tender tips are 'preferred';
- 164 g fresh / 39 g dried per litre as documented quantities;
- fresh basil ~24 g/L;
- claims about volatile compounds and drying losses;
- 'associated with wellbeing in traditional medicine';
- compounds listed as relevant to the finished beverage.

Future plant cards should clearly label:
**Documented in Chemo / Botanical identity / Sensory role / Other ethnobotanical context / Chemistry evidence**.

Disposition: MERGE into Level 2/3; do not preserve as a separate peer path unless the final whole-library synthesis establishes a reusable plant-reference archetype.

## 5. Schoolbook guide assessment

`chemo-schoolbook-guide.html` is designed for ordinary readers but contains instructional certainty that outruns the evidence layer.

Examples requiring quarantine:
- 'BEST: Young Tender Tips';
- 'AVOID: Yellow or Very Old Leaves';
- claims that young tips have the most aromatic oils;
- exact leaf weights treated as a standard recipe;
- causal 'why' explanations not clearly sourced;
- teaching format that makes interpretation look authoritative.

The accessible educational style may inspire future Level 1/2 presentation, but the current content cannot be migrated wholesale.

Disposition: ARCHIVE AS SOURCE MATERIAL / DO NOT MIGRATE CLAIMS WITHOUT VALIDATION.

## 6. Visual guide assessment

`chemo-visual-pictogram-guide.html` is a presentation mode, not an evidence layer. It repeats exact percentages, timings, leaf choices and flavour judgments in authoritative cards.

Future visual material must be generated only from approved LOOK/UNDERSTAND content and may not add a new proposition.

Disposition: RETIRE AS CURRENT GUIDE after verified replacement exists; preserve until migration is complete.

## 7. Message-engine risks requiring verification

### High priority

1. Study sample: 64 households and 16 key informants — exact scope and denominator.
2. 55.8% roasted method figure and other method percentages.
3. Claim that all 64 households use spices every time.
4. Which spices/herbs are universal versus common/optional.
5. Claim that Chemo is always served with food.
6. Claim that it is never stored/reheated.
7. Exact food pairings and whether source describes them as universal.
8. Claim that food moderates the brew's 'intensity'.
9. Leaf state/age used by each method and whether young tips are preferred.
10. 164 g fresh and 39 g dried leaf quantities per litre.
11. Exact herb/spice quantities.
12. Roasting time and target colour.
13. Whether crushing warm is directly documented and whether any proposed extraction advantage is measured.
14. Maillard reaction claim in actual coffee leaves under documented household roasting conditions.
15. Claims that roasted preparation is sweeter/more balanced and whether these are respondent reports.
16. Majang use of fresh-leaf method and exact source wording.
17. Gender/custodian claims about knowledge transmission.
18. Religious ceremony / communal activity claims.
19. Statements that wild coffee populations and evolutionary origin directly explain the practice.
20. Medicinal associations attached to individual herbs/spices.

### Rhetorical/interpretive claims to rewrite or label

- 'before the beans are sorted for export ... the leaves are already there';
- 'the drink that defines hospitality';
- 'no exceptions';
- 'what separates Chemo from every other coffee leaf tradition';
- 'the Maillard reaction transforms the leaf' as established fact;
- 'complete offering' unless directly sourced as community framing;
- 'most of the world treats as residue';
- 'knowledge tested and refined over many generations' unless documented;
- 'full plant is familiar' as causal explanation for invention of leaf beverages.

## 8. Structural/navigation problem

Current peer family routes:
- What is this?
- What's fascinating
- Go deeper
- Recipes
- Plants & Spices
- Schoolbook Guide
- Visual Guide

These are not seven levels of understanding. They are a mixture of depth, reference modules and presentation modes.

Rebuild family navigation:

**Chemo — Look**
→ **Understand Chemo**
→ **Evidence & Sources**

Inside UNDERSTAND or EXAMINE:
- Documented Preparation
- Plants & Spices

These should be modules/sections, not parallel competing doors unless the whole-library architecture later justifies reusable cross-family reference pages.

## 9. Ordinary-reader design requirements

### LOOK
- one clear description;
- location and source context in one line;
- maximum 3–4 verified distinctive facts;
- a simple preparation-flow diagram only if the documented method can be represented without invention;
- no chemistry diagram;
- no long spice list;
- no exact household percentages unless they materially aid understanding;
- one 'Understand more' route.

### UNDERSTAND
- cultural setting;
- preparation variants;
- hospitality/food context;
- concise plant/spice module;
- source/report labels next to relevant claims.

### EXAMINE
- full household-method table;
- botanical names;
- exact quantities;
- study methods and limitations;
- chemistry/hypotheses;
- bibliography.

## 10. Family rebuild outcome

Chemo should become a coherent three-depth path rather than seven peer pages.

**LOOK:** what it is.

**UNDERSTAND:** culture, hospitality, preparation variation, plants/spices.

**EXAMINE:** source methods, full ingredient record, evidence limits, chemistry and bibliography.

The preparation and plant content remain available but are subordinate to the depth model.
