Door Nine - After the cup

From the Cup to the Body

Before asking whether coffee leaf is useful to the body, we first need to understand what actually reaches the body.

The problemFour wordsExampleSources

It is easy to read that a coffee leaf contains mangiferin, chlorogenic acids or caffeine and then assume the drink delivers the same compounds to the body. It does not work that simply.

Door Four already covers the compounds and the way processing changes them. This page does not repeat that chemistry. It asks what happens after those compounds enter a drink.

The key point

A chemical analysis of a dry leaf is useful, but it cannot by itself tell us what dose a person receives or what effect, if any, follows.

For the earlier chemistry, see The Reactive Landscape and The Chemistry.

Four words that make the research easier to understand

Extraction

What comes out of the processed leaf and enters the drink.

Bioaccessibility

What becomes available during digestion and can potentially be absorbed.

Bioavailability

What actually reaches the bloodstream, often after being changed.

Bioactivity

Whether the amount that reaches the body produces a measurable biological response.

A simple example

Suppose a processed coffee leaf contains a high amount of chlorogenic acid. Brewing may extract only part of it. Digestion may change part of that. Gut microbes may convert some into other metabolites. The body may absorb those metabolites differently from the original compound.

This is why newer coffee leaf studies are looking at intestinal cells, gut microbes, short-chain fatty acids and bile acids instead of stopping at leaf composition.

Background sources: 2024 review, 2024 intestinal cell study, and 2026 animal study.

What is still missing

There is very little work tracing a defined coffee leaf drink all the way from cup chemistry through digestion to blood or urine metabolites in humans.
