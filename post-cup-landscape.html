Door Nine · The Post-Cup Landscape

The drinker is the final transformation environment.

The chemistry measured in a leaf is only the starting material. Brewing, digestion, microbial metabolism, absorption and host metabolism can change what is actually encountered by the body.

Four distinctions

Extraction

What moves from processed leaf into the beverage.

Bioaccessibility

What becomes available for absorption during digestion.

Bioavailability

What reaches systemic circulation and in what transformed form.

Bioactivity

What produces a measurable biological response at a realistic exposure.

Why this matters for coffee leaf

Coffee leaves can contain chlorogenic acids, mangiferin and other flavonoids and alkaloids, but their presence in the dry leaf does not demonstrate a human effect. Processing changes composition; brewing changes extraction; the gastrointestinal tract and microbiota can transform compounds again.

Working research model
dry leaf→finished cup→stomach / small intestine→colon microbiota→metabolites→circulation / tissues

Connection to the Reactive Landscape

Paper I asks what processing may be doing to multiple chemical reservoirs before and during the cup. The Post-Cup Landscape extends that question downstream: which transformed compounds remain available, which are metabolised by microbes, and which reach human tissues?
