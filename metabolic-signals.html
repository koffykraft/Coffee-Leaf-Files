Door Nine · Metabolic Signals

Mechanisms before claims.

Coffee-leaf studies report activity in carbohydrate-digestion, lipid, bile-acid and metabolic models. Most evidence remains in vitro or animal.

Carbohydrate digestion

In vitro

The 2026 Pu-erh-style pile-fermentation study measured α-amylase and α-glucosidase inhibition, glucose-diffusion retardation and starch digestibility. Fermentation pathway and duration changed the magnitude of these assays.

Lipid-related assays

In vitroAnimal

The same fermentation work measured pancreatic-lipase inhibition. Separately, the 2026 high-fat-diet mouse study reported changes in circulating triglycerides, total cholesterol and LDL along with liver lipid accumulation.

Bile acids, SCFAs and the enterohepatic axis

Animal

Recent animal work links coffee-leaf polyphenol-rich extracts with altered bile-acid and short-chain-fatty-acid profiles. These are mechanistically interesting because they sit between gut microbial activity and host metabolism.

Not established in humans

These findings do not justify describing coffee-leaf tea as anti-diabetic, weight-loss, cholesterol-lowering or a treatment for metabolic disease.

Processing is part of the biological question

The 2026 pile-fermentation study is especially useful because activity did not simply rise with “more fermentation.” Some measures peaked at intermediate stages. This fits Buna's broader principle that processing moves the leaf through different chemical states rather than merely preserving or destroying “good compounds.”
