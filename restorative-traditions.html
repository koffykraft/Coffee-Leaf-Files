Traditional descriptions of coffee-leaf beverages can be documented faithfully without treating them as clinical evidence.

Chemo

The 2026 Tepi study documents household preparation, spices, social use and cultural meaning. Participants described the drink in terms that include warming, refreshing, invigorating and restorative experience. Researchers did not establish a clinical restorative effect.

Engere and coffee-leaf brew

Recent Ethiopian documentation similarly records household use, perceived medicinal value and the social and food context of coffee-leaf drinks. Such reports belong in Buna as evidence of lived practice, not as proof of mechanism.

Buna convention

Traditional report: “People describe this drink as restorative.”
Scientific question: “What measurable physiological observations, if any, correspond to that experience?”
Scientific status: “Not yet established.”

Dotted lines, not arrows

Traditional terms such as warming, strengthening, refreshing, restorative and nourishing may generate modern research questions around stimulation, nutrition, hydration, gut physiology or stress response. They should not be retrofitted into modern diagnoses.
