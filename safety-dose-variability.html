Door Nine - Safety

Safety, Dose and Variability

Why a coffee leaf product must be understood as a real finished food or drink, not only as a plant name.

Why dose mattersVariabilityRegulationProcessingSources

Safety is not a property of the words coffee leaf. It depends on what leaf was used, how it was processed, how strong the drink is, how much is consumed and what else may be present in the finished product.

The detailed process work is already in Door Three. See From Leaf to Cup and Experimental Pathways. This page deals only with exposure and safety.

Why dose comes first

Two drinks made from the same plant can deliver very different amounts of caffeine or other compounds. A weak infusion and a concentrated extract are not the same exposure. A whole-leaf food is not the same exposure as an infusion.

Caffeine and other constituents can vary with species, cultivar, leaf maturity, climate, processing and brewing.

Practical rule

For a product intended for regular use, measure the finished drink or food whenever possible. The consumer receives the finished product, not an average value from a paper.

Source: Tritsch et al., Foods, 2022.

What the European rules are useful for

The European Union authorised coffee leaf infusion as a traditional food from a third country and set specifications for the finished infusion. Later rules expanded authorised uses.

How to use this information

The regulations provide a useful benchmark for defined coffee leaf preparations. They are not a universal recipe and do not remove the need to understand the actual product being made.

  • Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2020/917Coffee leaf infusion authorisation and specifications.
  • Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/931Extension of authorised uses.

Processing creates different safety questions

Fermentation

Time, moisture, sanitation, culture and unwanted microbial growth need control.

Smoking and roasting

Smoke and high heat create new chemistry. Finished-product testing is more useful than assuming that all smoke exposure is harmless.

Whole leaf foods

Eating the leaf gives a different exposure from drinking an infusion because material that would normally remain in the spent leaf is also consumed.

Source quality

Pesticide residues, heavy metals, microbial contamination and other hazards depend on where the leaf came from and how it was handled.

The main safety rule

Do not transfer a safety conclusion from one cultivar, process, extract or brew strength to another finished coffee leaf product without checking whether the exposure is comparable.
