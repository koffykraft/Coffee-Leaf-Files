Door Nine · Safety, Dose & Variability

Safety belongs to the finished product.

Species, maturity, processing, concentration, serving size, fermentation hygiene and contaminants all matter. A botanical name alone does not define exposure.

Caffeine is variable

Coffee-leaf caffeine can vary with species, cultivar, maturity, environment, processing and brewing. Any functional positioning should therefore start with measurement of the actual finished beverage rather than assuming a generic caffeine level.

European traditional-food specification

The EU authorisation for coffee-leaf infusion provides a useful regulatory reference point for defined preparation and finished-infusion limits, including caffeine and 5-CQA. This is a legal specification for authorised use, not a universal recipe or a clinical safety guarantee.

Processing-specific safety

Fermentation

Control culture, time, moisture, sanitation and unwanted microbial growth.

Smoking / roasting

Finished-product chemistry should be evaluated rather than assuming traditional smoke exposure is neutral.

Whole-leaf foods

Exposure differs from infusion because insoluble leaf material is consumed.

Contaminants

Pesticide residues, heavy metals, microbial load and mycotoxin risk depend on source and process.

Product-level rule

A finding from one extract, cultivar, fermentation or brew strength should not automatically be transferred to another finished coffee-leaf product.
