Door Nine · Gut Barrier & Microbiome

A strong emerging preclinical signal.

Recent coffee-leaf research has moved from cell models toward whole-animal studies of intestinal integrity, microbiota, microbial metabolites and the enterohepatic axis. Human benefit has not yet been established.

Animal2023

Coffee-leaf tea extract, microbiota and hyperuricemia nephropathy

Rat work reported microbiome and short-chain-fatty-acid changes alongside metabolic and organ-level outcomes. It provides an early whole-organism signal, not evidence that a beverage treats hyperuricemia in people.

Cell model2024

Intestinal barrier dysfunction in a Caco-2/U937 co-culture

Fractionation work found different coffee-leaf fractions behaved differently. Polyphenol-rich fractions showed the strongest protective signals, while other fractions could contribute to inflammatory signalling. This supports treating coffee leaf as a heterogeneous matrix rather than a single “anti-inflammatory” ingredient.

Animal2026

Barrier integrity, microbiota and enterohepatic axis

In high-fat-diet mice, a polyphenol-rich coffee-leaf fraction was associated with tighter-junction markers, lower intestinal leakage markers, altered bile acids and SCFAs, lower inflammatory markers and changes in microbiota including Akkermansia and Parabacteroides.

What Buna can say

Polyphenol-rich coffee-leaf extracts have produced repeatable signals involving intestinal integrity, inflammatory markers, microbial composition and microbial metabolites in preclinical models.

What Buna cannot say

Coffee-leaf beverages have not been shown to improve the human microbiome or treat gut disease.
