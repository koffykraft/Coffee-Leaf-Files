Door Nine - Research gaps

What We Still Do Not Know

A guide to the missing evidence, and why those missing pieces matter.

Where we areHuman questionsProcessingNext studySources

Coffee leaf research has moved beyond simply listing compounds. We now have processing studies, cell models, animal studies, traditional-use research and some human intervention work. The missing piece is a strong bridge between a clearly defined cup and a clearly measured human response.

Why the gaps matter

Recording unanswered questions is part of good research. It helps prevent promising early results from becoming claims before they have been tested properly.

Human questions that remain open

  • After a defined cup, which coffee leaf compounds or metabolites appear in human blood or urine?
  • Does regular consumption change the human gut microbiome in a repeatable way?
  • Do microbial changes correspond to useful metabolites or measurable human outcomes?
  • Can the 2025 cortisol finding be repeated in a larger controlled study?
  • How much do diet, microbiome and caffeine sensitivity change individual responses?

Processing questions that remain open

Door Three already documents the processing landscape. Door Nine adds a different question: what do those process differences mean after digestion?

  • Does fermentation improve the bioaccessibility of some compounds while reducing others?
  • How do green, oxidised, roasted, dark-fermented and smoked preparations compare after digestion?
  • What does smoke source contribute to aroma chemistry and safety?
  • How do maturity, species, cultivar, shade and season interact with processing?
  • How different are Arabica and Robusta drinks when matched for brew strength?

A useful bridge study

1

Define the raw material

Record species, cultivar, maturity, season and growing conditions.

2

Define the process

Record withering, oxidation, fermentation, heat, smoke, drying and storage.

3

Measure the cup

Measure the actual beverage rather than assuming the leaf composition.

4

Follow digestion

Use simulated digestion and microbial studies where appropriate.

5

Test the same drink in people

Measure human metabolites and carefully chosen outcomes.

Why this would help

It would connect plant, process, cup, digestion and person in one chain. That is the bridge the present literature mostly lacks.
