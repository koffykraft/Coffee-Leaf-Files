Door Nine - Research gaps

What We Still Do Not Know

The unanswered questions that matter most if coffee leaf research is to move from promising signals to useful human knowledge.

Where we areWhat is missingHuman studiesProcessingBest bridge study

The field is no longer empty. We have detailed chemistry, processing studies, intestinal cell models, animal studies, ethnographic work and an early human intervention study. The problem is that these pieces do not yet join into one complete chain.

The missing chain

Defined leaf -> defined process -> measured cup -> digestion and microbial transformation -> absorbed metabolites -> human response.

Most published work studies only one or two links. That is why strong conclusions are still difficult even though the individual studies are increasingly interesting.

What is missing between the existing studies?

We know a good deal about the leaf

Researchers can measure chlorogenic acids, mangiferin, caffeine, phenolics and many other constituents. But dry-leaf composition does not tell us exactly what enters a cup.

We know processing changes chemistry

Fermentation, oxidation and heat can change phenolics, pigments, aroma and laboratory activity. But we rarely know whether those process differences still matter after human digestion.

We have preclinical gut and metabolic signals

Cell and animal studies now connect coffee leaf extracts with barrier markers, microbes, short-chain fatty acids, bile acids, inflammation and lipid handling. But they mostly use extracts rather than ordinary beverages.

We have an early human signal

The 2025 study connects a defined coffee leaf preparation with sensory response and salivary cortisol. It does not yet tell us about repeated consumption, metabolites, gut effects or longer-term outcomes.

The most useful human questions

What actually enters the person?

After a measured cup, which parent compounds and metabolites appear in blood or urine, at what concentrations, and for how long?

Does the gut respond in people?

Do repeated servings change microbiome composition, short-chain fatty acids or barrier-related markers in a reproducible way?

Does processing style matter after drinking?

If green, oxidised, fermented or smoked coffee leaf drinks differ chemically, do those differences survive digestion and produce different human exposures?

Can the cortisol result be repeated?

A larger controlled study could test whether the 2025 signal is reproducible and whether it depends on time of day, caffeine, expectation, sensory preference or beverage chemistry.

What happens with ordinary repeated use?

Single servings and concentrated extracts cannot answer what happens when a normal coffee leaf beverage is consumed regularly over weeks.

The processing questions that matter most

Door Three maps the processing landscape. Door Nine asks which process changes survive the journey into the body.

  • Does fermentation make some compounds more bioaccessible while reducing others?
  • Do microbial fermentation products themselves survive into the beverage and matter after digestion?
  • How do green, oxidised, roasted, dark-fermented and smoked preparations compare when brewed to the same strength?
  • How much of the variation comes from process, and how much from species, cultivar, leaf age, shade or season?
  • For smoked products, what aroma compounds are created and what safety markers should be measured?
  • How different are whole-leaf foods from infusions when the insoluble leaf material is consumed?

A study that would connect the field

1

Start with a defined leaf

Record species, cultivar, leaf age, season, shade, farm and harvest conditions.

2

Use a repeatable process

Record withering, handling, fermentation, oxidation, heat, smoke, drying and storage.

3

Measure the actual drink

Analyse the beverage at the serving strength used in the human study, including caffeine and the compounds of interest.

4

Follow what digestion does

Use simulated digestion and microbial work to predict which compounds and metabolites may reach the intestine and circulation.

5

Give that same defined drink to people

Measure blood or urine metabolites, carefully chosen gut or physiological markers, sensory response and tolerability.

Why this would change the field

It would stop treating plant chemistry, processing, digestion and human response as separate subjects. It would let researchers say which features of a real coffee leaf beverage actually survive all the way to the person.
