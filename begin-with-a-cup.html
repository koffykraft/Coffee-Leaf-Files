Door One

Begin With a Cup

A gentle introduction to coffee leaf.

What is coffee leaf?

Coffee is not only a bean. The same plant also has leaves. In several places where coffee grows, those leaves have been dried, boiled, smoked or brewed and shared as their own beverage, separate from the bean entirely.

Where has it been used?

Coffee originates in the highlands of Ethiopia, where the plant still grows wild in forest understory. Leaf preparations such as Engere, Kuti and Chemo are documented there. A similar tradition, Kawa Daun, exists in the highlands of West Sumatra, Indonesia.

These are not the only places, and they are not presented as a list to memorise. They are starting points, each with its own page if you want to read further.

What does it taste like?

It depends on the leaf and how it is prepared. Some words come up often:

Green, herbal, sweetish, honey-like, floral, fruity, roasted, smoky, earthy.

If you want to know why, which compounds and which processing choices are involved, The Sensory Landscape of Coffee Leaf goes into more detail.

How is it different from coffee and tea?

It is not coffee without the beans, and it is not tea from the tea plant (Camellia sinensis). It is a different part of the coffee plant, with its own character. Sometimes it is closer to one, sometimes to the other, and sometimes it is its own thing entirely.

Why are we studying it?

Traditional use exists and is documented. Scientific interest in coffee leaf is growing. Compared with the bean, which has been processed and studied for centuries, the leaf's processing possibilities remain largely unexplored. That combination seemed worth taking seriously.
